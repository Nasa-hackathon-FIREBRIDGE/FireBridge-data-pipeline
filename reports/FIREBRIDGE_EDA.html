01The question

The project in one minute

When was burning unusual here — and how much of the apparent change comes from switching satellites?

Satellites see different-sized pixels and visit at different times. A more sensitive sensor can record more hotspots even when burning has not increased. FIREBRIDGE aims to make the record more comparable.

  • A comparable view Estimate activity on the Aqua reference scale in map squares about 5 km × 5 km.
  • A detailed view Keep the original VIIRS detections for local exploration.

Our starting area is the Chefchaouen–Larache rectangle in northern Morocco. It is a study box, not a province boundary. These outputs are the blueprint’s goals; they have not been built or validated.

03Reading the data

One row is one detected pixel, at one time

A single fire can produce many rows: across neighboring pixels, later passes, and different satellites. A hotspot count is not a count of fires or burned hectares.

Location35.17056°, -6.05516°Map latitude and longitude.

MW means megawatts: power, not burned area. Confidence categories differ by sensor; they are not a shared probability that a fire occurred.

No row does not mean “no fire.” The satellite may have seen no hotspot, had a cloudy view, or provided no usable observation. The point files alone cannot tell us which.

04What we learned

Explore patterns, then check what explains them

The Aqua reference is small: 57 detections on 27 dates in 2025. At 5 km and within 30 minutes, the S-NPP/Aqua diagnostic finds only 4 timestamp matches where both reported a hotspot.

Those matches are neither all observable pairs nor a model score. We need complete observation information and more contiguous years or area before evaluating a model. Quality checks also removed 2 exact duplicate pilot rows.

05The blueprint

How the data becomes FIREBRIDGE

  1. Collect detections and observation coverage

    Combine points with fire masks (clear, cloud, water or invalid) and geolocation (where every pixel lies). Masks and geolocation are still missing.

  2. Compare the same place and near-time passes

    Use common 5 km cells and only land both sensors could clearly observe. Keep insufficient observations marked unknown.

  3. Learn an Aqua-equivalent response

    Start with simple comparisons, then a simple statistical model. Keep a more complex model only if it does better on reserved data.

  4. Test beyond the training sample

    Reserve years and southern Spain. Hide Aqua during prediction, then reveal it for comparison. Aqua is an imperfect reference, not ground truth.

  5. Explain the result to users

    Show a calendar, source comparisons and supporting evidence, while keeping original VIIRS detail visible.

The model asks: “Would Aqua report a retained hotspot under these supported conditions?” It does not directly answer whether a true fire incident existed.

A 60-second team explanation

“FIREBRIDGE aims to help us compare observed burning over time and see how much the satellite choice affects the picture. We have explored millions of global rows and found 2,923 hotspot detections in our northern Morocco pilot. These are detected pixels, not individual fires. We can already map when and where each source reports activity. Next, we need to know what each satellite could clearly see, including times with no detections. Then we can compare shared observations, learn an Aqua-equivalent response, and test it on reserved data. The result should make comparisons easier while preserving VIIRS detail. We have a clear data plan; we have not yet proved the model works.”
Technical notes and data checks

The pilot rectangle is 34.5–35.9° N, −6.4 to −4.5° longitude; it is not polygon-clipped to land. Counts use valid, exactly deduplicated rows, including all confidence classes. Global raw counts retain duplicates; an independent check confirmed 304 exact duplicates in the 2024 Landsat archive.

The pairing diagnostic uses daytime nominal/high detections, standard streams, a 5 km EPSG:6933 grid and nearest timestamps without reuse within a sensor pair/cell. It contains both-positive samples only. It cannot establish false-positive rates, recall or model accuracy. The present files do not provide the observation denominator needed for the blueprint’s harmonized metrics.

The worked example comes directly from pilot_detections.csv. Counts and access status come from validation.json, pilot_platform_year.csv and science_download_manifest.json. Rebuild with firebridge_env/bin/python analysis/build_team_report.py.
